TiltAware / FIELD LAB 01
A small experiment in everyday motion

Your phone has
an angle. See it.

TiltAware turns your phone’s orientation sensors into a live visual story. Watch the angle shift, then test whether video, reading, and gaming change the way you hold your device.

One question, three activities. Does the task change how steadily you hold your phone? This is an orientation experiment—not a diagnosis of neck posture.
README — How to use
Quick setup: Use a modern mobile browser over HTTPS (GitHub Pages is HTTPS). Tap Enable sensors and grant access if prompted. If sensors do not start, open the page directly in Chrome or Safari rather than an in-app browser.
  1. Choose a comfortable starting hold. Sit or stand naturally, support the phone as you normally would, and keep your grip and viewing distance consistent between activities.
  2. Set the reference. After live readings appear, hold the phone in that starting position and tap Set reference. The phone preview and derived tilt angle will use that orientation as zero.
  3. Interpret the colors. Green means close to the reference, amber means a moderate difference, and red means a larger difference. These are configurable app heuristics, not a judgement of posture or health.
  4. Run fair comparisons. Use the same hand, similar viewing distance, the same duration, and the same reference for each activity. Repeat sessions if results look unusual.
  5. Reset with care. “Reset session” clears the current live sample window but does not erase saved activity results. Saved results remain in this browser until you clear them.

Important: TiltAware measures the phone, not your head, neck, spine, viewing distance, or muscle load. Device orientation axes and alpha availability vary by device and browser.

WHAT DO ALPHA, BETA, AND GAMMA MEAN?

These are the three angles reported by the browser’s device-orientation sensor. The diagram uses the common labels alpha (yaw), beta (pitch), and gamma (roll) to show the three kinds of rotation. Browser/device orientation and screen rotation can affect how the angles behave in practice; labels are a guide, not a promise that every device reports them identically.

⌖ Alpha (Z-axis) — yaw / rotationRotation around the axis perpendicular to the screen. Often resembles heading, but should not be treated as a reliable compass in every browser.
↕ Beta (X-axis) — pitchFront-to-back rotation. The beta value is colored against its own reference band.
↔ Gamma (Y-axis) — rollSide-to-side rotation. The sign and screen-relative interpretation may change with device orientation.

Conceptual guide, not a calibration diagram. TiltAware measures the phone’s orientation, not body posture. The derived tilt is calculated from beta and gamma relative to the saved reference; alpha is shown separately.

In the 16th century, the House of Habsburg controlled much of Central Europe, the Netherlands, and Spain, as well as Spain’s colonies in the Americas. The House was perhaps the first true world power, with the Sun always shining on some portion of it. Its ruler was also Holy Roman Emperor, and his seat of power was Prague. The Emperor in the late 16th century, Rudolph II, loved intellectual life. He invested in the arts, the sciences (including astrology and alchemy), and mathematics, making Prague a world center of learning and scholarship. It is appropriate, then, that in this learned atmosphere arose an early robot: the Golem of Prague.

A golem (GOH-lem) is a clay robot known in Jewish folklore, constructed from dust, fire, and water. It is brought to life by inscribing emet, Hebrew for “truth,” on its brow. Animated by truth, but lacking free will, a golem always does exactly what it is told. This is lucky, because the golem is incredibly powerful, able to withstand and accomplish more than its creators could. However, its obedience also brings danger, as careless instructions or unexpected events can turn a golem against its makers. Its abundance of power is matched by its lack of wisdom.

In some versions of the golem legend, Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel sought a way to defend the Jews of Prague. As in many parts of 16th-century Central Europe, the Jews of Prague were persecuted. Using secret techniques from the Kabbalah, Rabbi Judah was able to build a golem, animate it with “truth,” and order it to defend the Jewish people of Prague. Not everyone agreed with Judah’s action, fearing unintended consequences of toying with the power of life. Ultimately, Judah was forced to destroy the golem, as its combination of extraordinary power with clumsiness eventually led to innocent deaths. Wiping away one letter from the inscription emet to spell instead met, “death,” Rabbi Judah decommissioned the robot.

Scientists also make golems. Our golems rarely have physical form, but they too are often made of clay, living in silicon as computer code. These golems are scientific models. But these golems have real effects on the world, through the predictions they make and the intuitions they challenge or inspire. A concern with “truth” enlivens these models, but just like a golem or a modern robot, scientific models are neither true nor false, neither prophets nor charlatans. Rather, they are constructs engineered for some purpose. These constructs are incredibly powerful, dutifully conducting their programmed calculations.

READ THE SIGNAL, NOT THE MYTH

Interpretation guide
Time in target
The fraction of valid samples within the selected beta range. This is an angle-band statistic, not a clinical posture grade.
Movement variability
The spread of beta and gamma values during a session. More movement may simply mean more interaction or a different task.
Sustained deviation
Time spent outside the band for at least two consecutive seconds, with a small buffer to prevent rapid status flicker.
Missing data
If the browser stops delivering samples, the gap is excluded rather than treated as a safe reading.

HOW THIS EXPERIMENT WORKS

Transparent by design

01 / Capture

The page reads DeviceOrientationEvent alpha, beta, and gamma values when supported and permitted by the browser. Samples are timestamped locally.

02 / Classify

The live status uses the derived tilt difference from the reference, calculated from beta and gamma. The beta readout is independently colored against its own reference band. Amber indicates a moderate departure; red indicates a larger departure. These are project heuristics.

03 / Compare

Activity summaries use valid samples only. Variability is the root-mean-square spread across beta and gamma. Small sample counts should be interpreted cautiously.

Privacy: orientation data and session summaries are stored in this browser on this device. TiltAware does not upload readings to a server. Clearing browser site data may erase saved sessions.
