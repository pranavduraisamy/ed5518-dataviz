Read me first — how to use TiltAware
Quick setup: Use a modern mobile browser over HTTPS (GitHub Pages is HTTPS). Tap Enable sensors and grant access if prompted. If sensors do not start, open the page directly in Chrome or Safari rather than an in-app browser.
  1. Choose a comfortable starting hold. Sit or stand naturally, support the phone as you normally would, and keep your grip and viewing distance consistent between activities.
  2. Set the reference. After live readings appear, hold the phone in that starting position and tap Set reference. The phone preview and derived tilt angle will use that orientation as zero.
  3. Interpret the colors. Green means close to the reference, amber means a moderate difference, and red means a larger difference. These are configurable app heuristics, not a judgement of posture or health.
  4. Run fair comparisons. Use the same hand, similar viewing distance, the same duration, and the same reference for each activity. Repeat sessions if results look unusual.
  5. Reset with care. “Reset session” clears the current live sample window but does not erase saved activity results. Saved results remain in this browser until you clear them.

Important: TiltAware measures the phone, not your head, neck, spine, viewing distance, or muscle load. Device orientation axes and alpha availability vary by device and browser.

WHAT DO ALPHA, BETA, AND GAMMA MEAN?

Device-relative axes
Alpha (α) — heading / rotationRotation around the device’s vertical axis. It can behave like a compass heading; availability and reference direction vary.
Beta (β) — front/back tiltForward/backward rotation of the device. The live beta value changes color as it leaves the selected band.
Gamma (γ) — side-to-side tiltLeft/right rotation of the device. Exact sign and coordinate convention can differ across devices and browsers.

The graphic is conceptual, not a calibration diagram. Browser orientation values are not identical to a physical inclinometer and should not be interpreted as body angles.

The first thing you notice about a familiar object is usually its shape. The second is the way you hold it. A phone seems almost weightless, but small adjustments happen constantly: a thumb reaches for the screen, a wrist turns, the top edge dips by a few degrees.

This experiment is interested in those small changes. It does not know where your head is, how far away the screen sits, or whether your shoulders are relaxed. It only sees the orientation of the device itself—and asks whether different tasks leave different traces.

Keep reading at your natural pace. There is no perfect score to chase. The useful result is a comparison made under similar conditions, with the assumptions left in plain view.

READ THE SIGNAL, NOT THE MYTH

Interpretation guide
Time in target
The fraction of valid samples within the selected beta range. This is an angle-band statistic, not a clinical posture grade.
Movement variability
The spread of beta and gamma values during a session. More movement may simply mean more interaction or a different task.
Sustained deviation
Time spent outside the band for at least two consecutive seconds, with a small buffer to prevent rapid status flicker.
Missing data
If the browser stops delivering samples, the gap is excluded rather than treated as a safe reading.

HOW THIS EXPERIMENT WORKS

Transparent by design

01 / Capture

The page reads DeviceOrientationEvent alpha, beta, and gamma values when supported and permitted by the browser. Samples are timestamped locally.

02 / Classify

The live status uses the derived tilt difference from the reference, calculated from beta and gamma. The beta readout is independently colored against its own reference band. Amber indicates a moderate departure; red indicates a larger departure. These are project heuristics.

03 / Compare

Activity summaries use valid samples only. Variability is the root-mean-square spread across beta and gamma. Small sample counts should be interpreted cautiously.

THRESHOLD SETTINGS

Adjustable heuristic
±15°

A wider band marks more readings as within target. Choose a value and keep it constant across activities.

+15°

Red begins this many additional degrees beyond the target band, or after a sustained deviation. These settings affect future and live classifications.

Privacy: orientation data and session summaries are stored in this browser on this device. TiltAware does not upload readings to a server. Clearing browser site data may erase saved sessions.
